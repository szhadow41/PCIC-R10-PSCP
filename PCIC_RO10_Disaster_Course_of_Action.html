EXECUTIVE BRIEF & DECISIONS REQUIRED — FOR THE MANAGEMENT MEETING

Purpose of this brief: RO10 needs an executable disaster course of action grounded in the PCIC PSCP (2nd Edition). Analysis of the plan against RO10 conditions shows the sequence is fixed by the Recovery Time Objectives, and five gaps must be decided by management now — the rest of this document is the supporting design.

Finding 1 — The recovery sequence is not negotiableInformation Technology & General Services (Tier 2) must recover within 12–24 hours; only then can claims and underwriting (Tier 4) recover within 48–72 hours. Plans that skip Information Technology/data restoration fail both tiers.
Finding 2 — Claims lead recovery, not underwritingAfter a calamity, Mission Essential Function 2 (indemnity claims) must get first call on restored capacity — affected farmers are watching PCIC's response. Underwriting backlog follows immediately to close uninsured gaps.
Finding 3 — Five roles make or break Day 1Regional Manager (command) → Communications/Marketing and Sales Division (call tree & reports) → Information Technology Specialist (PCIC Automated Business System/policy data) → Medical/Claims Adjustment Division (life safety) → Logistics/Administrative and Finance Division (hot site & supplies) — each with a confirmed alternate within 2 hours.

1. PURPOSE, SCOPE & REGIONAL HAZARD PROFILE

1.1 Purpose

This document translates the national PCIC Public Service Continuity Plan (PSCP) into a regional-level course of action for PCIC Regional Office X, so the Regional Office knows exactly who acts, what is restored first, and in what sequence during and after a disaster. It provides:

  • A phase-based operating design keyed to the PSCP's three scenarios (first 24 hours; 2–7 days; 8–14 days);
  • Specific recommendations at the 24-hour, 72-hour, and 120-hour marks;
  • A ranked list of essential resources to prioritize for recovery;
  • The Regional Office-level role players (per PSCP Table 2) with a prioritized list of vital roles; and
  • Ready-to-use inputs for tomorrow's planning session.

1.2 Scope

Applies to all RO10 units and its extension offices, covering the two Mission Essential Functions and two Essential Supporting Activities defined in PSCP Section VIII:

#FunctionClassificationRecovery Time ObjectiveDesign Consequence
Mission Essential Function 1Processing of Application for Insurance Coverage (underwriting: receipt, encoding, inspection, approval, premium, issuance of policy)Tier 4Within 48–72 hoursResume after Information Technology/services are restored; seasonal deadlines matter
Mission Essential Function 2Processing of Claims for Indemnity and Other Benefits (notice of loss, verification, investigation, Claims Adjustment and Verification Report, approval, payment release)Tier 4Within 48–72 hoursPriority Mission Essential Function after a disaster — affected farmers need indemnity [PROPOSED]
Essential Supporting Activity 1Information Technology Services (internet and local area network, servers, PCIC Automated Business System, PCIC Financial Management System, Human Resource Management System, shared drives, helpdesk)Tier 2Within 12–24 hoursMust recover FIRST — Mission Essential Functions cannot run without it
Essential Supporting Activity 2General Services (facility management, finance/supplies/equipment, transport, records handling)Tier 2Within 12–24 hoursEnables everything else: power, transport, supplies, records
Key planning insight: The recovery sequence is fixed by the Recovery Time Objectives — Essential Supporting Activities (12–24 hours) recover before Mission Essential Functions (48–72 hours). Any 24-hour plan that does not restore Information Technology and general services has already failed its Tier 2 objectives, and the 72-hour Mission Essential Function targets become unreachable.

5.3 VITAL ROLES TO PRIORITIZE — "First Five Within Two Hours"

If only five roles can be filled in the first two hours (personnel may be scattered, roads cut, power out), fill them in this order:

  1. 1  Regional Manager / Quick Response Team Head (command) — without a commander there is no coordinated response, no activation recommendation, no external coordination. Alternate: Administrative and Finance Division Div. Chief or designated Regional Manager officer-in-charge.
  2. 2  Communications Coordinator (Marketing and Sales Division Chief) (information) — owns the call tree, the Disaster Control Group record of decisions, initial reports to Head Office, and public messaging; every other role is found through this role. Alternate: Insurance Underwriter II.
  3. 3  Information and Communications Technology and Records Safety Coordinator (Information Technology Specialist) (data survival) — PCIC Automated Business System/policy & claims data and backups face a 12–24 hours Recovery Time Objective; data lost in the first day cannot be reconstructed before farmers arrive with claims. Alternate: Information Technology staff.
  4. 4  Medical Coordinator (Claims Adjustment Division Chief) (life safety) — casualty assessment, medical referral, and the personnel-welfare record; also the Claims Adjustment Division chief is the pipeline owner for the priority Mission Essential Function (claims). Alternate: Adjuster II.
  5. 5  Logistics Coordinator (Administrative and Finance Division Chief) (restoration) — hot site, power, supplies, transport, and finance/procurement authority; the only role that can physically stand up an alternate workplace. Alternate: Administrative Services Officer.

Next in line: Facilities Safety Officer (structure/safety gate) → the three Quick Response Team member division chiefs → Human Resource focal → remaining division chiefs (Mission Essential Function manpower).

Design principle [PROPOSED]: rank roles by (1) command, (2) information flow, (3) irrecoverable data loss, (4) life safety, (5) restoration capacity — mirroring the resource priorities in Section 4. Every ranked role must have a named, pre-identified alternate in the contact directory before the next hazard season.

6.2 Seven Recommendations for the RO10 Planning Team

  1. Designate and pre-stage the hot site now. The single biggest Level-4 dependency is an untested alternate site. Choose the extension office, verify its internet/power, and pre-position a generator, Information Technology kit, and records chest before the next typhoon season.
  2. Treat data as mission-critical, not Information Technology-routine. Adopt daily off-site/cloud backup of PCIC Automated Business System and PCIC Financial Management System with a monthly restore test. The 12–24 hours Information and Communications Technology Recovery Time Objective is meaningless without a verified restore path.
  3. Pre-write the first 24 hours as checklists, not prose. One-page cards per coordinator (roll call script, Initial Damage Assessment form, Situation Report template, advisory template) — memory fails in the first hours.
  4. Adopt claims-first triage for disasters. Document a standing rule: after any regional calamity, Mission Essential Function 2 (claims) gets first call on restored capacity, with fast-track handling for farmers in areas under a local state-of-calamity declaration.
  5. Cross-train for single-point-of-failure roles. Information Technology Specialist, Marketing and Sales Division Chief, and Administrative and Finance Division Chief roles each need at least two trained personnel; aligns with PSCP staffing-continuity strategy (backup personnel + cross-training).
  6. Bind the call tree to an Alternative Work Arrangements decision tree. When staff are notified, they must simultaneously learn their mode (report / Work From Home / standby / hot site). Separate memos cause confusion.
  7. Start the post-incident report at 72 hours, not after deactivation. Assign Information and Communications Technology, Facilities, and Quick Response Team members to draft in parallel; the RO10 lessons-learned register becomes the input to the next PSCP revision.
